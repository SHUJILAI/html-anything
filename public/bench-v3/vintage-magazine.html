Your calendar is a black hole. Meetings multiply without warning, deep work disappears, and you end each day feeling simultaneously exhausted and unaccomplished. The worst part? The data backs it up. Not with reminders or color-coded blocks you have to drag around yourself, but with an engine that understood the cadence of your work and quietly carved out the time you needed — before anyone else could claim it.

The answer is Lumen v0.4, and at its core is a feature we call Auto-Blocks.

The Problem We Set Out to Fix

Most calendar tools treat your time as a passive container. Meetings drop in, blocks get pushed, and the gap between intention and reality widens every week. Founders in our beta described the same cycle: Sunday night optimism about deep-work sessions, Monday reality of back-to-back calls, and a creeping guilt that followed them into the weekend.

The problem isn't discipline. It's architecture. When your calendar has no mechanism for self-defense, every new meeting invite is an asymmetric fight — the meeting always wins because it has a time, a room, a sender. Your creative work has none of those things. It exists only in your intentions.

"When your calendar has no mechanism for self-defense, every new meeting invite is an asymmetric fight — the meeting always wins."

We needed to give deep work the same structural weight as a scheduled call. That meant building something that acted first, not reactively.

What's New: Auto-Blocks

Auto-Blocks is an on-device engine that reads your meetings and working hours, then automatically reserves 90-minute deep-work slots before your calendar fills up. No manual scheduling. No friction. No anxiety-inducing Sunday evening review. Just protected time, placed where it will actually hold.

The mechanics are deliberate by design:

  • Real-time scanning — The engine watches your calendar and availability as they change, not once at setup.
  • Heuristics + a small on-device model — It learns when you tend to do your best thinking and favors those windows. Everything runs locally, so your calendar data never leaves your device.
  • First-mover locking — Blocks are placed proactively, giving them structural priority before invites arrive.
  • Slack snooze sync — When a block is active, Slack notifications pause automatically. No manual status-setting required.

The on-device architecture deserves a word of its own. We made a deliberate choice not to process your calendar data on our servers. Founders' schedules are sensitive — who you're meeting with, how often, when you're free. That information stays on your machine.

Everything Shipping in v0.4

Auto-Blocks is the headlining feature, but it ships alongside a suite of additions that make the experience complete rather than partial:

  • Auto-Blocks engine — Heuristic scheduling combined with a small on-device model. Privacy-first by architecture, not by policy.
  • Slack snooze sync — Your focus blocks automatically silence Slack. No status updates, no manual toggles, no explaining yourself.
  • iOS widget — A glanceable view of your next block and a countdown timer, so you know exactly how long you have before the world re-enters.
  • Team mode — Lets teammates see when your blocks are active without exposing meeting titles or attendee lists. Respect for focus without sacrificing coordination.

Team mode in particular came directly from beta feedback. Founders managing small teams wanted to protect their own focus without creating opacity that frustrated their colleagues. The solution — showing block status but not meeting content — threads that needle cleanly.
