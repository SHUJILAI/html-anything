Lumen v0.4 — the calendar that defends your focus before meetings steal it.

Lumen Engineering · June 2025 · 8 min read

Every calendar starts honest. A few recurring standups, a weekly review, maybe a 1:1 or two. Then, slowly, the creep begins. A stakeholder sync here, an "alignment call" there, and before long the workday is a mosaic of 30-minute gaps — none of them long enough to think, let alone build anything that matters. We built Lumen to solve exactly this problem, and today, with v0.4, we are shipping the most ambitious piece of that vision: Auto-Blocks, an on-device scheduling engine that claims your deep-work time before the rest of the week can.

This is not a simple "block your mornings" heuristic. Auto-Blocks reads your existing meetings, understands your stated working hours, and places 90-minute protected slots in the gaps that actually exist — recalculating every time your calendar changes. It runs entirely on-device. Your meeting titles and attendees never leave your machine.

What we learned from 210 founders

Before writing a single line of the Auto-Blocks engine, we spent three months interviewing early users. The finding was consistent: the problem was not a lack of intention. Founders knew they needed deep-work time. They just couldn't defend it. Meetings metastasize in ways that feel individually reasonable but collectively catastrophic. A reschedule here, a quick sync there — and by Thursday the week is gone.

We ran a closed beta with 210 founders across six weeks. Before Auto-Blocks, the median participant was getting 4.2 hours of self-reported deep-work time per week. After six weeks with Auto-Blocks enabled, that number rose to 11.7 hours — a 178% increase. Meeting overrun rate dropped from 38% to 12%. Perhaps most telling: those who reported "feeling productive today" jumped from 41% to 78%. That last number is the one we care about most. Shipped hours are vanity. Felt efficacy is the signal.

"I stopped doing calendar tetris at 9pm on Sundays. Lumen just... handles it. I open Monday already defended."

That quote came from a founder running a 14-person team. She is not a power user. She does not have a personal assistant. She connected her Google Calendar, enabled Auto-Blocks, and got out of the way.

How the Auto-Blocks engine works

The engine runs a two-phase scheduling pass. In the first phase, it reads your calendar events and constructs a gap map — every contiguous free interval longer than 90 minutes within your working hours. In the second phase, a small on-device ML model scores each gap by a combination of factors: time of day relative to your historical productivity patterns, meeting density immediately before and after the slot, and day-of-week preference signals collected over time.

The model is deliberately small. It was trained on aggregate, anonymized scheduling patterns but runs inference entirely locally, using Core ML on iOS and a lightweight ONNX runtime on the web client. There is no round-trip to a server. There is no queue. The block appears in your calendar within seconds of a sync.

When a new meeting invitation lands or an existing event shifts, the engine re-evaluates and, if necessary, moves the block. You will see the update the next time you open Lumen or on the next background sync, whichever comes first. You can drag, shorten, or delete any Auto-Block manually — Lumen treats that as a signal and learns from it. 

{
  "type": "auto_block",
  "duration_minutes": 90,
  "score": 0.87,
  "signals": {
    "time_of_day_preference": 0.91,
    "meeting_density_buffer": 0.82,
    "day_of_week_weight": 0.88
  },
  "slack_snooze": true,
  "editable": true
}

Every Auto-Block event carries a score and the signals that produced it. These are not surfaced in the UI by default, but they are available in the developer export if you want to inspect how the engine is thinking.

The rest of v0.4

Auto-Blocks is the headline, but three other features shipped alongside it.

Slack snooze sync.

When an Auto-Block goes active, Lumen sets your Slack status to Do Not Disturb and snoozes notifications for the duration. When the block ends, your status resets automatically. No Zapier, no IFTTT, no manual toggling. It connects via Slack's standard OAuth scope and requires only the dnd:write permission.

iOS widget.

A small-size home screen widget shows your next Auto-Block — title, start time, and a countdown. A medium widget adds the day's remaining blocks in a list. Tap either to jump directly into the block detail view. It uses WidgetKit and updates on a standard timeline refresh, so it is always within a few minutes of accurate.

Team mode.

Team mode makes Auto-Blocks a coordination primitive. Managers can see a shared view of which team members have active blocks, and when, without seeing any meeting titles or attendee lists — just the protected intervals. It changes the culture of meeting scheduling: before you send an invite, you can see that two of your engineers are in focus blocks for the next hour and a half. You might decide to send an async message instead.

Getting started in five steps

Setup is intentionally minimal. Download or log in to Lumen on web or iOS, then grant calendar access so Auto-Blocks can read your meetings and working hours. Enable Auto-Blocks in settings — the engine will run its first pass immediately and place blocks within seconds. Review the first week of scheduled blocks and adjust anything that does not fit; each edit is a training signal. Finally, connect Slack to activate the notification snooze sync.

Supported calendars at launch: Google Calendar, Outlook, and Apple Calendar. Additional integrations are on the roadmap. Block duration is fixed at 90 minutes in v0.4; custom durations are the single most-requested feature in our beta survey and are slated for v0.5.

A note on privacy

Calendar data is sensitive. Meeting titles carry strategy, personnel decisions, and deal status. Auto-Blocks was designed from the start with the constraint that meeting content never leaves the device unencrypted. The on-device model sees event durations and timestamps — not titles, not attendees, not video links. When data does reach our servers (for account sync and Team mode coordination), it is encrypted in transit and at rest, and the payload contains block intervals only, not underlying event metadata.

Team mode shares block intervals with teammates, not calendar events. A team member can see that you are in a deep-work block from 10:00 to 11:30. They cannot see that you are in "Q3 board prep with investor relations." That boundary is enforced at the data layer, not just the UI.

What comes next

v0.5 will focus on three things: custom block durations (the community has been loud and we heard it), a redesigned preferences UI that makes the ML signals legible and adjustable, and deeper team analytics so managers can see focus-time trends across their organization over weeks, not just days.

The longer-term roadmap includes native integrations with Linear, Notion, and GitHub — the idea being that Auto-Blocks could one day be seeded not just from calendar whitespace but from the actual work that needs doing. If you have three P0 issues open in Linear, the engine should know that and schedule accordingly. That is a bigger problem, and we are not rushing it. But it is where this is going.
